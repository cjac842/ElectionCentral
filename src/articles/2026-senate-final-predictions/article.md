## Background

The 2024 Senate Election saw Republicans take back the chamber with a 53-47 majority, flipping former Democrat turned independent Joe Manchin’s West Virginia seat, Jon Tester’s Montana seat, Sherrod Brown’s Ohio seat and Bob Casey’s Pennsylvania seat. This might seem bad but Democrats avoided the worst that night, as they held onto seats in Nevada, Arizona, Wisconsin and Michigan, all states that President Donald Trump won.

Now, Democrats try to take back the chamber by not only trying to flip classic battleground North Carolina and Susan Collins’ Maine seat, but also trying to flip double digit Trump states such as Iowa, Ohio, Texas, Alaska, and Kansas. Let’s take a look at each race starting with the battleground states Democrats have to defend.

## Democratic Must-Holds

### Minnesota: Peggy Flanagan (D) vs. Michele Tafoya (R)

![Peggy Flanagan](peggy-flanagan.png) ![Michele Tafoya](michele-tafoya.png)

A race that probably shouldn’t be this close in this environment but it is largely due to Governor Tim Walz’s issues and Peggy Flanagan being the current Lt. Governor. Also, Tafoya has definitely fundraised well and kept it close in spending…. but with this environment it probably isn’t enough for her to defeat Flanagan.

**Rating: Likely D**

### New Hampshire: Chris Pappas (D) vs. John E. Sununu (R)

![Chris Pappas](chris-pappas.png) ![John Sununu](john-sununu.png)

For a moment this race looked like it was going to be very competitive. John Sununu, the older brother of former Governor Chris Sununu and former Senator himself, was polling neck and neck against Representative Chris Pappas. However, it looks like Pappas opened up a lead on Sununu and isn’t looking back. Not the right year to run John.

**Rating: Likely D**

### Georgia: Jon Ossoff (D-Inc.) vs. Mike Collins (R)

![Jon Ossoff](jon-ossoff.png) ![Mike Collins](mike-collins.png)

Senator Jon Ossoff was supposed to be in a tough race this year… after all, Donald Trump won his state in 2024. However, due to a MASSIVE fundraising haul and nearly a 26-1 cash on hand advantage, he’s opened up a pretty comfortable 7pt-9pt polling lead over his challenger, Representative Mike Collins. Jon Ossoff likely doesn’t have to worry about losing reelection this year and now is being heralded as a potential 2028 Democratic Presidential Candidate.

**Rating: Likely D**

### Michigan: Abdul El-Sayed (D) vs. Mike Rogers (R)

![Abdul El-Sayed](abdul-el-sayed.png) ![Mike Rogers](mike-rogers.png)

Michigan’s Senate seat has been the center of attention this cycle for many reasons such as the combativeness between the two candidates but also due to the sharp ideological rift within the Democratic primary itself. Dr. Abdul El-Sayed, a progressive former health official, narrowly won the primary against moderate Representative Haley Stevens by 0.9%, despite leading in the polling by double digits. After a primary heavily focused on issues such as Israel and “progressive vs. moderate”, party unity is a challenge for El-Sayed, as he aims to consolidate support among skeptical moderate and working-class Democrats. Meanwhile, former Republican Congressman Mike Rogers is polling within striking distance of El-Sayed. An El-Sayed victory would greatly boost the progressive wing of the Democratic Party, while a loss would be a major setback.

**Rating: Lean D**

## Democratic Must-Flips

If Democrats want to flip the Senate this November, they MUST win these two seats.

### North Carolina: Roy Cooper (D) vs. Michael Whatley (R)

![Roy Cooper](roy-cooper.png) ![Michael Whatley](michael-whatley.png)

If you told people in early 2025 that the North Carolina Senate race wouldn’t even be competitive right now in favor of the Democrats, they’d call you crazy. Former Governor Roy Cooper seemingly has put the race to bed against former RNC chair Michael Whatley. He’s heavily expected to flip the seat being vacated by Senator Thom Tillis (R).

**Rating: Likely D (flip)**

### Maine: Troy Jackson (D) vs. Susan Collins (R-Inc.)

![Troy Jackson](troy-jackson.png) ![Susan Collins](susan-collins.png)

Democrats have been yearning to flip this seat for YEARS. Maybe this will be the year they finally defeat the political unicorn in Susan Collins, the only Republican Senator in a state Kamala Harris won in 2024. To defeat her, Democrats are running a replacement candidate in former Maine Senate President Troy Jackson because the nominated Democratic candidate, Graham Platner, got exposed as a Nazi rapist. This has been quite the… “interesting” race… but if Democrats win, it’s a win… let’s just forget how they got there.

**Rating: Lean D (flip)**

## The Majority Makers

If Democrats hold everything they have and flip NC+ME, they’re at 49 seats. They’d need two more to get to 51. Out of these three, they’d need to win two of them.

### Alaska: Mary Peltola (D) vs. Dan Sullivan (R-Inc.)

![Mary Peltola](mary-peltola.png) ![Dan Sullivan](dan-sullivan.png)

Senator Dan Sullivan was elected in 2014 narrowly by defeating incumbent Democrat Mark Begich, who won in 2008. After dispatching independent Al Gross in 2020, he faces a strong challenge from former U.S Representative Mary Peltola (D), who lost her reelection narrowly in 2024 to Nick Begich III (yes he’s Mark Begich’s nephew… and also a Republican). Pelola put up a strong performance in the primary and has led narrowly in the polls, but this one is coming down to the wire.

**Rating: Tilt D (flip)**

### Ohio Special: Sherrod Brown (D) vs. Jon Husted (R-Inc.)

![Sherrod Brown](sherrod-brown.png) ![Jon Husted](jon-husted.png)

Just two years ago, then-Senator Sherrod Brown, the final statewide elected Democrat in Ohio, lost reelection to Bernie Moreno, a used cars salesman, as Donald Trump carried the state by around 11pts. Two years later, he’s aiming for a comeback to the Senate by attempting to flip the seat that Vice President J.D Vance vacated. He’s facing Senator Jon Husted, who was appointed by Governor Mike DeWine (R) to serve in place of Vance. Brown has led in the polling, even into the final days when typically the polls start to skew Republican, but still, Ohio polling has been known to underestimate Republicans by a few points… this one could be closer than we expect.

**Note:** The winner of this seat will have to run again in 2028 for the full term if they choose to do so, so this is only a 2 year “rental” seat.

**Rating: Tilt D (flip)**

### Texas: James Talarico (D) vs. Ken Paxton (R)

![James Talarico](james-talarico.png) ![Ken Paxton](ken-paxton.png)

Oh boy this one had A LOT of drama behind it. Both James Talarico and Ken Paxton emerged from nasty and contentious primaries, with Talarico beating Congresswoman Jasmine Crockett and Attorney General Ken Paxton defeating INCUMBENT Senator John Cornyn, making Cornyn the first Texas Republican Senator to ever lose their nomination, and the first time that an incumbent Texas Senator was defeated in a primary since 1970.

Paxton has been scandal plagued throughout his career, notably being impeached by the Republican controlled Texas House of Representatives, and has been under a lot of scrutiny for his actions by Talarico, who running a campaign of anti corruption, the economy, and anti-data centers (mostly a Governor Greg Abbott issue).

If Talarico wins he’ll become the first Democrat to win a Texas Senate seat since 1988, and the first to win this particular seat since 1960, when then Senator Lyndon B. Johnson won reelection while simultaneously being elected Vice President. Talarico winning requires a lot to go right… but this is the year that everything *can* go right.

**Rating: Tilt D (flip)**

## Bonus Opportunities

These two races, Democrats are not favored to flip but they have a shot.

### Iowa: Josh Turek (D) vs. Ashley Hinson (R)

![Josh Turek](josh-turek.png) ![Ashley Hinson](ashley-hinson.png)

U.S Representative Ashley Hinson (R) and State Representative Josh Turek (D) are locked in a surprisingly competitive race. While Iowa has strongly leaned Republican in recent cycles, the race has tightened due to rising costs and the state being hit hard by the Trump tariffs. Turek's candidacy has also benefited from strong momentum generated by Democrat Rob Sand's strong polling numbers in the Iowa governor race. This one could be decided by the Governor race. If Rob Sand is crushing Republican Zach Lahn, then Ashley Hinson could be in a lot of trouble. If Sand is narrowly winning or losing, Hinson should be in better shape.

**Rating: Tilt R**

### Kansas: Adam Hamilton vs. Roger Marshall (R-Inc.)

![Adam Hamilton](adam-hamilton.png) ![Roger Marshall](roger-marshall.png)

Kansas is a state if you told someone a month ago it would be close, they’d call you crazy. Seemingly, it has gotten super competitive, as incumbent Republican Senator Roger Marshall is struggling with voter frustration with him in the state, and Adam Hamilton, the pastor of the Methodist Church of Resurrection (the largest Methodist congregation in the entire country), is mounting a strong campaign. Still though, Kansas is a red state that hasn’t sent a Democrat to the Senate since 1932. Something even crazier, no Democrat has won this seat by solely the popular vote… EVER IN EXISTENCE. The last time a Democrat represented this seat, legislatures were still electing Senators and the 17th amendment didn’t exist yet (Kansas had a system where there was a popular vote, but the legislature still had the final say in electing the Senator). So Hamilton is trying to defy gravity in Kansas, but it may be too much.

**Rating: Lean R**

### Nebraska: Dan Osborn (I) vs. Pete Ricketts (R-Inc.)

![Dan Osborn](dan-osborn.png) ![Pete Ricketts](pete-ricketts.png)

Dan Osborn, after mounting a strong campaign against Nebraska’s other Senator, Deb Fischer, is giving another crack at it against Senator Pete Ricketts, who won a Special Election in 2024 on the same ballot as Fischer. Ricketts, unlike Fischer, is not asleep at the wheel this time, and has been working hard knowing the challenge Osborn presents. Osborn may get close but very unlikely he wins.

**Rating: Lean R**

## Honorable Mentions

Democrats are not favored to flip these but these deserve being talked about.

### South Carolina: Annie Andrews (D) vs. Darline Graham (R-Inc.)

![Annie Andrews](annie-andrews.png) ![Darline Graham](darline-graham.png)

This race wasn't on the radar much until Senator Lindsey Graham (R) passed away suddenly in July. Now his younger sister, Darline, who was appointed by Governor Henry McMaster (R) to serve out the rest of his brother's term, is running for a full term to be South Carolina's U.S Senator. However, it definitely hasn't been an easy road for Darline. After being appointed Senator, her political "noviceness"... has shown. Most notably in a debate where she said she didn't know much about foreign policy despite her older brother being a foreign policy "guru". After surviving the special primary, she's been under a lot of scruitiny in South Carolina, and the polls show a tight race... but so did 2020... and we know how that turned out...

**Rating: Lean R**

### Florida Special: Angie Nixon (D) vs. Ashley Moody (R-Inc.)

![Angie Nixon](angie-nixon.png) ![Ashley Moody](ashley-moody.png)

Even if Democrats have a big night, they still may not even get that close in the race to replace Secretary of State Marco Rubio. Florida nowadays is just such a conservative hub... but it will be interesting to see if there's any reversions with voting blocs such as Latinos in south Florida. Nixon will NOT win, but her performance will definitely be an indicator of how Florida, and potentially the rest of the night, will go.

**Rating: Likely R**
